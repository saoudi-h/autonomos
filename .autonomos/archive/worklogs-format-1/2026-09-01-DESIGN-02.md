## Owner calibration — first review

The owner identified that the first comparison combined too many variables at
once. `Control` was intended as a generic baseline, not a fourth candidate, but
the label made that distinction unclear. More importantly, the candidates
changed layout, visual language, and framing copy together. The underlying
publication fixture was shared, but the different headline treatments mean the
comparison is not a fully controlled test of visual variables.

Observed feedback:

- Candidate A has the strongest provisional information architecture: a useful
  sidebar and a clear operational layout. Its palette is coherent but
  intentionally divisive and is not accepted as Herald direction.
- Candidate B has an attractive editorial surface and generally careful prose,
  but its layout is wrong for this operator workspace. Its rounded, bold,
  uppercase badges are a strong visual mismatch.
- Candidate C is not inherently broken, but its route-map metaphor and palette
  feel out of subject and evoke an amateur 2010s pharmacy interface. It offers
  no useful direction to retain at this stage.
- All four views use the recurring AI-looking pattern of uppercase overlines
  with expanded letter-spacing. This is a high-confidence anti-default signal
  for the next controlled test, not yet a universal project rule.

Method correction for the next run:

1. Choose the information architecture and dominant job with neutral,
   low-fidelity content skeletons.
2. Apply small isolated studies for density, navigation, typography, geometry,
   status encoding, color, and material rather than composing full styles at
   random.
3. Compose one direction only after those decisions have a recorded state.

The next candidate should default to sentence case, avoid expanded tracking and
uppercase labels unless explicitly justified, and avoid pill badges as the
universal status grammar. These rules remain provisional until they survive
another controlled Herald render and, where practical, a second surface.

## Owner rejection — H-05

The owner rejected H-05 as a design result. It was not technically broken or
ugly, but it was flavorless, hard to identify as a product, and not a result
that the design process should accept. The owner explicitly noted that it
should not satisfy the design director either.

The supplied Postiz, Mixpost, and OpenPost captures exposed what H-05 lacked:
each reference makes the category legible at first glance through concrete
objects and a dominant work surface. Calendar, channels, posts, media,
destination previews, and creation actions are visible before a viewer studies
the visual details. Each also has a memory hook that is more specific than a
font, radius, or color token.

Diagnosis of the failure:

1. H-05 treated `Humanist sans`, `Linework`, and `Dark console` as sufficient
   design decisions, then assembled them into a generic shell.
2. Anti-default rules became a negative identity: removing pills, shadows,
   radii, and tracking produced restraint but no positive point of view.
3. The Queue fixture described the domain in prose but did not make Herald's
   distinctive relation — one `Post` becoming multiple `Publications` in
   different `Channels` — visually unavoidable.
4. The result hid the category anchors that comparable products expose:
   calendar context, channel identity, post/media objects, and the next
   publication action.

H-05 is therefore marked rejected, not a failed implementation to polish.

## Method correction — identity before tokens

The next design pass must answer grounded questions before asking for style
preferences:

- What should a stranger recognize in five seconds?
- Which product object is central?
- Which relationship must be visible rather than explained by helper text?
- Which real content types provide the visual rhythm?
- What single, product-specific move creates memory without becoming a
  template?

The references are used for constraint extraction, not copying: expose the
category through real nouns, make the dominant workflow visible, use concrete
channel/media evidence, and give the product one defensible visual memory hook.
The method must then test the positive identity and the anti-default warnings
together. A sparse result is not automatically a good result.

## Owner checkpoint — H-09 and reference comparison

The owner considers both H-09 grammars successful, with the surface-led
candidate materially more finished and much less immediately recognizable as
AI-generated. The important gain is not the absence of every line; it is that
the interface now has a point of view rather than a collection of outlined
rectangles.

The supplied references refine the rule:

- Omarchy Plugin Marketplace uses borders extensively, but its lines belong to
  a coherent technical material: a dark catalog, a coordinate-like grid,
  plugin thumbnails, metadata, and hard-edged controls. The technique is not
  generic merely because it uses borders.
- Tala Web contains personal projects and imagery, yet its repeated dark
  portfolio vocabulary — micro-labels, tracked category lines, outlined tags,
  modular cards, and a warm CTA — still exposes a familiar AI-era grammar.
  Personal content alone does not make a surface distinctive.

The correction is therefore not “ban borders.” It is: every repeated visual
device needs a product, material, or interaction reason, and the system must
avoid combining several high-frequency AI signals without a specific thesis.
This is accepted as a provisional design-direction rule for the next holdout,
not yet as global Autonomos guidance.
